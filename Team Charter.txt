Ways of Working

Git workflow
- `main` is protected — no direct pushes
- All work arrives via feature branches and pull requests
- Every PR must be reviewed and approved by at least one other member before merging
- Branch naming: `feature/<short-description>` or `fix/<short-description>`
- Each milestone is a tagged release: `m1`, `m2`, `m3`, `m4`, `v1.0`

GitHub board
- Every task is an Issue on the GitHub Projects board
- Every issue has exactly one owner and a milestone label
- Issues are moved across columns: **Backlog → In Progress → In Review → Done**
- Scrum/Release Lead is responsible for keeping the board current each week

Commits
- Every member commits meaningful work every week from their own GitHub account
- Commit messages are clear and specific: `Add forwarding unit for EX/MEM hazard` not `update stuff`
- Commits and PRs are individual evidence — you will be asked to explain yours at defences

Weekly sprint
- Sprint planning: start of each week, Scrum Lead assigns issues
- Sprint note submitted with each milestone: what was done, what was not done, what is next, who did what (one page)
- Milestone submission: release-tag link + milestone PDF + sprint note, on Google Classroom by Sunday 23:59

Responsibilities and Expectations

1. Every member attends all three oral defences (D1, D2, D3). An unexcused absence scores zero for that defence — individual marks.
2. Every member appears or speaks in the YouTube demonstration.
3. Every member can explain their own commits, their own code, and the ISA decisions made in their role week.
4. If a member cannot meet a deadline, they notify the team at least 24 hours in advance and the Scrum Lead reassigns the work.
5. AI tools may be used for exploration and drafting. All use must be declared in `AI_USE.md` (tool, purpose, what you changed). Code you cannot explain at a defence is not credited as yours.
6. No changes to team composition after D1 without written approval from the coordinator.
